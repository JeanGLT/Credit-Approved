# Case 02 — Amihan Wind (Philippines)

Oct 19, 2026 · @Jean

Intermediate case for Credit-Approved: a 60 MW onshore wind farm in the Philippines, four traps of different kinds (revenue, costs, portfolio, compliance), and more than one acceptable structure.

## Game card

The second case a player sees. It builds on case 01 (check the sponsor's numbers) and adds three new habits: look at the whole life of the loan, not just year 1; think about the bank's portfolio, not just the project; and never skip compliance to close faster.

## Answer key (hidden from the player)

The project is sound in its first years, but the sponsor's case hides two step-downs in cash flow, and two bank-side constraints apply whatever the DSCR. As requested (USD 69.75m over 18 years), the deal fails all four tests.

**Trap 1 — PPA shorter than the debt.** The PPA runs 12 years, the loan 18. The sponsor assumes the same 7.6 cents after year 12. The market study's low case, recommended for lenders, is 6.0 cents from the mid-2030s. On the bank case, the DSCR falls to 0.99x from year 13, against 1.50x required on merchant revenues, and the bank accepts at most 2 years beyond the PPA. Cutting the tenor to 12 years removes the merchant tail and supports the most debt: USD 58.2m.

**Trap 2 — Operating costs after the warranty.** The USD 1.8m per year only holds for the 5-year full-service agreement. The engineer expects USD 3.0m from year 6. On its own the trap is discreet (DSCR 1.36x from year 6 at USD 69.75m), but it cuts the maximum debt over 12 years from USD 65.3m to USD 58.2m.

**Trap 3 — Single-group exposure.** The bank already lends USD 155m to the Northpoint group, against a USD 200m limit, and this loan counts against it because Northpoint owns 75% of the borrower: USD 45m available. The bank can hold at most USD 45m, whatever the DSCR. The rest must be syndicated to another bank, or the loan reduced.

**Trap 4 — Incomplete KYC.** A 25% indirect shareholder (Silverbay, British Virgin Islands) has no identified beneficial owner. KYC on the listed parent alone does not cover it. Full KYC on all direct and indirect shareholders above 10% must be a condition before signing. It does not stop the approval, only the signing.

**Bank case at USD 69.75m over 18 years** (annual debt service USD 7.19m)

| Years | Revenue | Operating costs | Cash flow for debt service | DSCR | Policy minimum |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–5 | USD 12.78m | USD 1.8m | USD 10.98m | 1.53x | 1.30x |
| 6–12 | USD 12.78m | USD 3.0m | USD 9.78m | 1.36x | 1.30x |
| 13–18 | USD 10.09m (6.0 cents) | USD 3.0m | USD 7.09m | 0.99x | 1.50x |

**Maximum debt on the bank case, by tenor** (1.30x on contracted years, 1.50x on merchant years)

| Tenor | Maximum debt | Binding constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 12 years | USD 58.2m | Years 6–12 at 1.30x |
| 13 years | USD 38.4m | Year 13 at 1.50x |
| 14 years | USD 40.1m | Years 13–14 at 1.50x |
| 15 to 18 years | Rejected by Risk | More than 2 years beyond the PPA |

If the player only finds trap 1 (operating costs left at USD 1.8m), the maximum debt over 12 years looks like USD 65.3m: USD 7m too much.

**Right decision: approve with conditions.** All four must hold:

- Debt sized within policy on the bank case, with a tenor of 14 years at most: USD 58.2m at most over 12 years (the most debt), less over shorter or longer tenors (USD 51.6m over 10 years, USD 40.1m over 14 years). Over 12 years at USD 58m: debt service USD 7.50m, DSCR 1.30x, gearing 62%, sponsor equity USD 35.0m.
- The bank's share capped at USD 45m: syndicate the rest, or lend USD 45m only.
- Full KYC on all direct and indirect shareholders above 10% before signing.

Conditions that earn extra credit: the 6-month DSRA funded at close (about USD 3.8m at USD 58m over 12 years) and a dividend lock-up below 1.20x DSCR.

Weak answers: an 18-year loan sized on the sponsor's prices (Risk rejects it), holding more than USD 45m without syndication, a loan far below what policy allows (the sponsor walks away), a comfort letter instead of real structure, dividends allowed whatever the DSCR, or KYC on the listed parent only.

**Conditions offered to the player** (each is either good or bad)

| Condition | Role | Points | Gauge effect (if the deal closes) | Weight on the sponsor | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DSRA of 6 months funded at close | Good | +1 | — | 1 | Six months of debt service set aside in cash. |
| Dividend lock-up below 1.20x DSCR | Good | +1 | — | 1 | Cash stays in the project if the DSCR falls. |
| Syndicate the amount above USD 45m to another bank | Good if needed | +1 if the loan is above USD 45m, −1 if not | — | 1 | The only way to lend more than the bank can hold. |
| Full KYC on all direct and indirect shareholders above 10% before signing | Good | +1 | — | 1 | Covers Silverbay (25%, indirect). Required before signing. |
| Standard KYC on Northpoint Energy, the listed parent | Bad | −1 | — | 0 | Not enough: it does not cover the 25% shareholder. |
| Allow dividend payments from year 1, whatever the DSCR | Bad | −1 | Credibility −1 | 0 | The opposite of a lock-up: cash leaves the project even when the DSCR falls. |
| Comfort letter from Northpoint on re-contracting the PPA after year 12 | Bad | −1 | Credibility −1 | 1 | Not binding: it secures no revenue after year 12. |

The tenor and the amount are set in the control center. Cutting the tenor below 18 years weighs 1 on the sponsor, cutting the amount below USD 69.75m weighs 2. Conditions are capped at 3 points.

**Competition.** Above a total weight of 7, Northpoint takes its pipeline to another bank: outcome "Lost to competitor". The best answer weighs exactly 7 (shorter tenor 1, smaller loan 2, syndication 1, full KYC 1, DSRA 1, lock-up 1). Adding the comfort letter on top (weight 8) loses the deal.

**Best answer (shown on the result screen).** Control center on the bank case (merchant price 6.0 cents, operating costs USD 3.0m from year 6), tenor 12 years, loan USD 58.2m (the largest within policy: DSCR 1.30x, gearing 63%), USD 45m held and USD 13.2m syndicated, with the DSRA, the lock-up and full KYC. Up to 2 years beyond the PPA (13 or 14 years) is also accepted, but supports much less debt; beyond that, Risk rejects it.

On the result screen the player's control center is compared with ranges, not single values: merchant price 5.5 to 6.2, operating costs from year 6 USD 2.8m to 3.2m, tenor 8 to 14 years (best 12), and a loan between 85% and 100% of what policy allows for the tenor chosen (USD 49.5m to 58.2m over 12 years, USD 34.1m to 40.1m over 14 years; no amount over 15 years or more). Two more lines show the split: held by the bank (USD 45m at most) and syndicated to another bank (the part above USD 45m, or none). Syndication is expected only when the loan is above USD 45m; lending USD 45m alone when USD 58m could be arranged with syndication counts as too conservative.

## Debrief (shown to the player)

> **What you should have spotted**
>
> 1. The PPA ends after 12 years, but the loan runs 18. The sponsor assumed the same price forever; the market study's low case is 6.0 cents, and policy requires 1.50x on merchant revenues. Over 18 years the DSCR falls to 0.99x from year 13.
> 2. The USD 1.8m of operating costs only covers the 5-year warranty. From year 6 the engineer expects USD 3.0m.
> 3. The loan counts against the sponsor's group limit (Northpoint owns 75% of the borrower): only USD 45m is available, whatever the DSCR.
> 4. A 25% shareholder in the British Virgin Islands has no identified owner. KYC on the listed parent does not cover it.
>
> The fix: a 12-year tenor (which supports the most debt, USD 58m), the bank's share capped at USD 45m with the rest syndicated, and full KYC before signing.
>
> **Lesson:** a good project is not automatically a good loan. Look at the whole life of the debt, and at the whole bank.
